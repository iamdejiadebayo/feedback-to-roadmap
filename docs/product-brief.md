# Product brief

## Problem hypothesis

A product manager at a small B2B software team receives feedback from interviews, support tickets, and surveys. Reviewing it across separate files makes it difficult to connect roadmap proposals to original evidence. We hypothesize that a lightweight review workspace can help them make clearer prioritization decisions.

This is a hypothesis, not a validated customer finding. The initial target segment and workflow should change if research contradicts them.

## Target user and job

Primary user: a product manager or founder at a small B2B software company who already collects feedback in spreadsheets.

Job: “When I prepare a planning discussion, help me organize customer feedback and explain the evidence and assumptions behind my proposed priorities.”

Secondary reviewer: an engineering or design partner who challenges grouping, feasibility, and scope.

## Value proposition

Move from scattered comments to a reviewable set of opportunities without losing the words customers actually used. Preserve disagreement and uncertainty rather than generating an authoritative-looking answer.

## First research questions

- Describe the last time customer feedback changed your roadmap. What happened?
- Where was the feedback stored? How did you organize and share it?
- How did you distinguish a frequent request from a strategically important one?
- What evidence did colleagues ask for? What was missing?
- How much manual categorization did you do, and where did it break down?
- What would prevent you from trusting automated theme suggestions?
- What would you need to remove or anonymize before using a tool like this?

Ask for specific recent examples before presenting the prototype. Avoid leading questions such as “Would AI make prioritization easier?”

## Proposed validation

Recruit 3–5 PMs or founders for exploratory interviews, then run a small usability study with fictional or permissioned, anonymized feedback. This is a proposed research plan; no participants have been interviewed yet.

Look for evidence that the problem is repeated and consequential. Observe whether people understand the grouping, correct it, and defend a priority. Record contrary evidence and reasons not to use the tool.

## Success measures to establish

- Time to produce a reviewed brief compared with the participant's current approach.
- Whether participants can find the source supporting a theme without help.
- Proportion of group assignments accepted or corrected by a reviewer.
- Whether users distinguish estimates from observed facts.
- Whether a participant chooses to use the workflow again on a real planning task.

No baseline or target improvement has been measured yet. Set targets after initial research.
